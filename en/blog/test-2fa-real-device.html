Which tool for testing 2FA on a real device?

Two families of tools run tests on real phones: hosted device farms, and devices sitting on your own network. They answer different constraints, and the choice comes down to what your authentication app requires.

By Sylvain Perez, creator of Q-Bot. Updated 27 August 2026

The two families

What are the two families of tools?

On one side, hosted device farms, where you rent access to phones that live at a provider. On the other, devices sitting on your own network, driven from your side. The first family brings numbers and variety, the second brings proximity. Many teams end up needing both.

Both run code on real hardware, which is already the main thing. The difference lies elsewhere: in where the device sits, and therefore in what it can reach.

  • a hosted farm gives access to dozens of models and versions, with no hardware to manage
  • a device on your network sees what your network sees, and nothing leaves your premises

Neither is better in the abstract. They answer two different constraints, and many teams need both.

The cloud

What does a hosted device change?

It brings coverage: many models, many Android and iOS versions, all available on demand and with no hardware to manage. It is the right answer when the question is « does my application render correctly everywhere », and that question matters a great deal.

It is the compatibility-testing tool, and nothing else replaces it. Two things to check once an authentication step enters the journey:

  • the device must be able to reach your test environment, often behind a private network
  • the authentication app must be enrolable on that device, and stay enrolled between sessions

These are logistics questions, not objections: they can be solved, but they should be asked before the choice, not after.

Your network

What does a device on your own network change?

It is enrolled once and stays enrolled, it reaches your internal environments with no gateway, and nothing it displays leaves your premises. That is the answer when authentication is itself part of the test, and when a security review has to sign off.

The account used for testing stays enrolled on a device that does not move, and that is what makes the journey replayable every night with nobody watching.

This is Q-Bot's model: an ordinary Android phone, wired over USB, driven through ADB, on your network. Test data stays inside the box, a subject covered by the security and compliance guide. To place this approach among the others, see the overview guide on automating 2FA.

The list to take with you

Which six questions should you ask a vendor?

They are not about advertised features but about what actually blocks in practice. Ask them of any vendor, including us: the answers will tell you within five minutes whether the tool covers your whole journey or only a part of it. Ask for them in writing.

Ask them as they stand:

  • can the device reach my internal test environment?
  • does my authentication account stay enrolled between sessions?
  • can the tool approve a pushed request, or only type a code?
  • can it read a displayed code and hand it back to my test?
  • where are my scenarios' screenshots stored?
  • which platforms exactly, and which ones NOT?

Our answer to the last one is on the record: Android only, no iOS. The driving relies on ADB, which has no equivalent on iPhone. If your journey has to be validated on an iPhone, a hosted farm is the right tool, not ours.

Other tools are named here only to place the families. We attribute no limitation to any named vendor: the six questions exist so that you get their answers from them.
