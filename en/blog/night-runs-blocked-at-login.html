Why your night runs stop at login

The morning report shows a cluster of failures, all in the same place, with the same trace. It is almost never a flaky suite: it is a step nothing could clear overnight. This guide covers the symptom, the usual workarounds, and what it takes to reach the end.

By Sylvain Perez, creator of Q-Bot. Updated 27 August 2026

The symptom

What does the symptom look like in a report?

A cluster of failures sharing three traits: there are many of them, they all land at the same point in the journey, and they carry the same trace. A genuinely flaky suite fails in a scattered way; this one fails with a regularity that should raise a flag.

The three signs that separate a blockage from instability:

  • failures are clustered on one step, not scattered through the suite
  • the trace is identical from one scenario to the next
  • the scenarios that never go through sign-in do pass

When those three signs line up, looking for the cause inside the suite is wasted time: the cause is upstream.

The cause

Why is it almost always the second factor?

Because it is the only step in the journey waiting for a human gesture. Everything else automates: typing, navigation, assertions. An approval on a phone does not: it waits for someone, and at three in the morning there is nobody.

A browser driver acts on the rendered document. As soon as the step leaves the browser it has no hold: no selector reaches a phone's screen. That is not a weakness of the tool, it is its scope, and the reference page sets out that division.

The four families of second factor do not pose the same problem either: a computed code can be recomputed, an approval request cannot. That distinction is drawn in « automating 2FA without a shared secret ».

The workarounds

What do the three usual workarounds cost?

Someone on night duty is expensive and not sustainable for long. An account without a second factor makes tests pass but stops testing the real journey. And skipping the step empties every assertion that comes after sign-in of its meaning.

All three, with their bill:

  • someone on duty: it works for a week, not a quarter, and it is paid in hours
  • an account without 2FA: quick, but the journey tested is no longer the users'
  • skipping the step: the run goes green again and no longer proves the same thing

The second and third are defensible in some cases: the guide on disabling 2FA in testing says which, and which not. The first one can be costed, and that is the subject of the guide on the cost of the manual step.

What it takes

What does it take for a run to reach the end?

That the step is cleared with nobody there, on the real journey. That means a device which stays enrolled between runs, one that is reachable from your pipeline, and a command the test can trigger itself at the right moment. All three can be checked before buying anything.

Three conditions, and they can be checked before buying anything:

  • a device enrolled once that stays enrolled from one night to the next
  • a device that reaches your test environment with no gateway
  • a triggerable command from the pipeline, at the moment the test needs it

The two families of tools that meet those conditions are compared in « which tool for testing 2FA on a real device ».
