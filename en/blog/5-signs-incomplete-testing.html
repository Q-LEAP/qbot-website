5 Signs Your Automated Tests
Don't Cover Enough

Your CI runs every night — yet bugs keep reaching production. These 5 red flags reveal blind spots in your QA strategy.

  1. Home
  2. ›
  3. Blog
  4. ›
  5. 5 Signs Your Automated Tests Don't Cover Enough
March 5, 2025 6 min read Q-Leap Team

You have a test automation suite. It runs nightly in CI. Green checkmarks everywhere. And yet, week after week, bugs keep reaching production. Users report issues on flows you were convinced were covered.

This paradox is far more common than it seems. An apparently healthy test suite can hide critical blind spots. Here are 5 concrete red flags that indicate your QA strategy is letting too many risks slip through — and what you can do about it.

Sign #1 — Your Tests Skip Authenticated Flows

If your automated tests cannot execute the full login flow of your application — especially one involving two-factor authentication (2FA) — entire sections of your software are going completely untested.

And yet, that is exactly where the most critical bugs hide. Features sitting behind an authentication gate are, by definition, the most sensitive: account management, financial transactions, access to personal or business data. Testing only public-facing pages is like inspecting the facade of a building without ever going inside.

Ask yourself this: can your test scripts log in to your application exactly as a real user would, including validating a 2FA code via a physical token or a mobile app? If the answer is no, the majority of your application is not being tested at all.

Sign #2 — Significant Areas of the App Have Zero Coverage

Open your code coverage report. If entire pages, modules, or user flows show 0% coverage, you are flying completely blind on those parts of the application.

The most commonly forgotten areas are those considered "secondary" or "low-traffic": admin panels, payment flows, document signing, complex business validation workflows. These are, ironically, the exact areas where a failure has the highest real-world impact.

A coverage report does not tell the whole story — it measures which lines of code were executed, not whether the underlying business behaviours were actually verified. But zones sitting at 0% are an immediate warning signal that demands investigation. Common uncovered areas include:

  • Admin panels and back-office interfaces
  • Payment and checkout flows
  • Document signing and e-signature workflows
  • Account creation and onboarding journeys
  • Multi-step validation and approval processes

Sign #3 — Your Tests Don't Reflect Real User Behaviour

Tests that simulate button clicks programmatically, bypass login forms via direct database injection, or substitute every network call with a mock — these tests provide a false sense of security.

A real user navigates your application with their own data, in their own browser, in their own sequence. They encounter intermediate states, network latency, and edge-case validation errors that your mocks simply do not replicate faithfully.

A test suite that relies too heavily on mocks and technical shortcuts can validate that the code "runs" without ever confirming that the user experience actually works. Heavily mocked suites test the mock, not the system. If your tests do not reproduce the genuine end-to-end actions of a real user — including the real authentication interaction — they will not catch the bugs that user will encounter in production.

Sign #4 — Your Regression Suite Skips Critical Paths

A regression suite that does not begin with "log in and perform the most important action in the application" is only testing secondary flows. It detects peripheral regressions but lets failures at the heart of your product's value go unnoticed.

Critical paths that absolutely must be covered in every regression cycle include:

  • Full login with two-factor authentication (2FA), including failure scenarios
  • High-value transactions and irreversible actions
  • Electronic document signing and approval workflows
  • Access to sensitive data after successful authentication
  • Critical state changes (validation, rejection, case closure)

If any of these scenarios is missing from your regression suite, every deployment carries an unmeasured and unquantified risk. A regression suite that skips critical paths is a false safety net — it gives the team confidence it has not earned.

Sign #5 — Your CI/CD Pipeline Stops at the 2FA Gate

This is the most serious signal of all. If your continuous integration pipeline cannot handle two-factor authentication, your continuous testing stops exactly where the critical portion of your application begins.

Every commit is deployed without having been tested beyond the login screen. In practice, this means your releases are only partially validated — and the only real coverage your authenticated flows receive is from your users in production. They become your QA team by default, and that is not a position any engineering team should be comfortable with.

This is not a skills problem. It is a tooling problem. Frameworks like Selenium or Playwright were not designed to interact with physical 2FA devices — USB tokens, smart cards, or apps like LuxTrust. The solution cannot come from a software workaround or an environment flag. It requires an approach capable of driving the full authentication flow exactly as a human tester would — including pressing the physical button.

How to Fix These Blind Spots

The good news: these gaps are identifiable and fixable with a structured approach.

Audit your coverage metrics critically. Do not stop at global percentages. Dig into which functional flows are actually being exercised, and which are not. A project with 80% line coverage can still have entire user journeys with zero test coverage.

Map your critical user journeys end-to-end. Identify the 5 to 10 scenarios whose failure would have the highest impact on your users and your business. These scenarios must be covered as a priority and verified on every release — no exceptions.

Invest in 2FA automation. This is often the primary blocker. Solutions like Q-BOT make it possible to automate two-factor authentication — including via physical devices or mobile apps like LuxTrust — and integrate it directly into your CI/CD pipeline without modifying your existing infrastructure. When your pipeline can handle 2FA, the entire authenticated surface of your application becomes testable.

Combine levels of testing. Unit tests verify internal logic; integration and end-to-end tests verify complete user journeys. Both are necessary and neither replaces the other. A project with only unit tests will still have untested user flows, no matter how high the coverage percentage looks.

Build coverage reviews into your process. Test coverage degrades naturally as an application evolves. New features ship, tests are not always added. Make reviewing uncovered areas a regular part of your team's retrospectives, not an annual audit.

Conclusion

A genuinely solid QA strategy tests the complete user journey — from the first public page all the way through the most critical authenticated actions, including every authentication step along the way. Teams that achieve full automation of this journey, 2FA included, ship faster, with greater confidence, and with significantly fewer production incidents.

If any of the 5 signs described here sounds familiar, it is time to address the blind spot. Production is not a testing environment — even if it often ends up playing that role.
