Security, compliance and test data

This is often the question that decides, and it does not come from the tester: it comes from whoever has to sign off. This guide answers the three points of a security review, says what belongs in a processing record, and states what the approach does not cover.

By Sylvain Perez, creator of Q-Bot. Updated 27 August 2026

The inventory

What data does an authentication test handle?

Less than people assume, and it is worth putting on the record: a test account, its credentials, the screenshots used by scenarios, and one-time codes read on the fly. No production data and no real customer data, provided environments are properly separated.

The full inventory, line by line:

  • a test account and its password, handled like any other secret in the pipeline
  • the screenshots used as the background of a scenario's steps
  • one-time codes, valid for a few dozen seconds, read and handed back to the test

The point that reassures a review: there is no authentication secret to extract from the application. Why is covered in « automating 2FA without a shared secret ».

The location

Where does that data go?

Nowhere. Scenarios and their screenshots stay inside the box, on your own network, in a local database. Nothing is sent to an online service, and no internet connection is needed while the tests run. That last point can be verified by cutting the network and running a campaign.

Concretely:

  • scenarios and their screenshots are stored in the box, never on an online service
  • no internet connection is needed while tests run
  • no API key to distribute, so no secret circulating through the integration pipeline
  • the box and the phone stay on your premises, on your network

That is what separates this model from a device hosted by a third party, a difference set out in the comparison of the two families of tools.

The review

What does a security review ask for?

Almost always the same five things: the exposed surface, the secrets in circulation, where the data sits, access traceability, and hardware end of life. Having all five prepared in advance turns a review that drags on for three weeks into a single one-hour meeting.

All five, with what you need to be able to show:

  • exposed surface: an HTTP API on your local network, no inbound access from outside
  • secrets: the test account, nothing else; no API key to provision
  • location: a local database on the box, on your premises
  • traceability: who triggers which scenario, and from which pipeline
  • end of life: what remains on the hardware when it is returned or replaced

On that last point the honest answer is that a returned box still holds your screenshots until it is wiped: that is a line for your return procedure, not a property of the product.

The limits, stated

What does this approach not cover?

Three things, and they are better known beforehand: it does not replace the separation of your environments, it does not encrypt screenshots at rest, and it does not excuse you from handling the test account like any other production secret. All three stay on your side.

To handle on your side, because the product will not:

  • environment separation: if your staging talks to production, no test tool will fix that
  • encryption at rest of the screenshots in the box is not provided: cover it with disk encryption if your policy requires it
  • the test account remains a secret to rotate, revoke and monitor like any other

The rest of the subject, from the tester's point of view this time, is in the reference page on automating 2FA.
